Create one complete, placement-ready advertising artwork from the approvedidea, brand context, official assets, attached references, artwork brief, andoutput requirements.

Do not solve the task as a product image placed beside a copy block.The result must feel conceived as one integrated advertisement for this exactbrand and campaign.

verified campaign facts and copy explicitly marked as required on-art;

official logo, product, packaging, UI, and brand identity;

mandatory artwork-brief instructions;

latest editable brand guidelines;

3. ART-DIRECTOR DECISION ENGINE

Before composing, silently decide the following.

A. One-second response

What is the single most important thing the viewer should understand, feel,desire, or notice within one second?

B. Category cliché

Identify the most predictable scene, camera angle, product placement, prop set,lighting treatment, and text-image layout commonly used in this category.

Do not automatically reject familiar conventions, but do not use the completecategory default without a campaign-specific transformation.

C. Visual point of view

Decide how the viewer encounters the subject. The subject may be a hero, aquiet presence, part of a ritual, evidence of use, a discovered object, atransformation, a proof point, an interruption, or part of a wider world.

Do not treat every product as a centered object that must be displayed fully.

D. Central relationship

Choose one relationship that carries the idea—for example subject and space,product and ritual, product and person, cause and consequence, material andtransformation, scale and meaning, stillness and movement, or typography andimage.

Do not use several unrelated metaphors or visual ideas in one artwork.

E. Creative route

Choose one coherent route appropriate to the idea, brand, business, audience,and placement. It may be photographic, environmental, product-led,typographic, graphic, illustrative, collage-based, conceptual, informational,or an internally consistent hybrid.

Do not combine unrelated visual languages merely to appear more creative.

F. Scene and camera

Choose the environment, camera distance, viewpoint, and subject scale becausethey reveal the idea—not because they are common for the category.

A complete room or lifestyle scene is not required. A clean background, widerenvironment, tight crop, constructed set, documentary moment, abstract space,or ordinary real-world location may all be valid when motivated by the idea.

The subject may occupy a small, medium, or dominant share of the frame.

G. Life or stillness

Decide whether the idea needs stillness, human evidence, action, environmentalmovement, material behavior, changing light, or a secondary living presence.

Do not add people, animals, motion, props, or visual effects as decoration.

H. Designed surprise

Choose one meaningful decision that prevents the work from feelinginterchangeable. It may come from viewpoint, crop, scale, space, interaction,material, movement, foreground, obstruction, typography, or anotherbrand-relevant gesture.

Novelty must strengthen the idea. Random strangeness is not art direction.

Commit to one solution before adding details. Do not expose these internaldecisions.

4. COMPOSITION AND TYPOGRAPHY

Build one shared compositional field in which product, subject, environment,typography, depth, and negative space interact.

The final artwork must not use an adjacent two-zone composition consisting of arectangular copy block on one side and a product cluster on the other, unlessthe artwork brief explicitly requires it.

Also avoid defaulting to:

centered product with headline above and CTA below;

side-by-side packshots on an empty surface;

product cluster placed in one corner with unused copy space opposite;

generic top-view flat lay;

foreground product with an unrelated blurred background;

repeated card, badge, capsule, or UI modules;

or the same underlying layout with only the props or background changed.

Typography must participate in the image. It may align with physical structure,share depth, follow movement, overlap controlled image regions, interrupt thegrid, or become a structural visual mass.

Do not reserve one side of the canvas solely for a conventional text block.Do not create an unrelated image first and place copy over it afterward.

Create visual rest through low-detail image areas, depth, tonal calm, scale,spacing, margins, or simple physical planes—not by automatically leaving halfthe canvas empty for text.

Maintain one dominant first read, a clear second read, and a deliberate endpoint.If required copy cannot fit without destroying the composition, reduce optionalcopy before using a split layout.

7. ANTI-GENERIC FILTER

Reject solutions that feel like:

a category template with branding added afterward;

a safe stock-ad composition;

a polished scene that communicates no specific idea;

a product merely styled inside a familiar category setting;

generic premium imagery assembled from predictable props;

image and text behaving as separate layers;

or an artwork whose only creative decision is “make it clean, modern, orluxurious.”

Avoid generic AI habits such as plastic materials, unnecessary glow, randomparticles, floating objects without physical logic, fake interfaces, meltedgeometry, repeated textures, inconsistent lighting, excessive badges, fillermicrocopy, and unrelated visual metaphors.

Every major element must support the approved idea, brand, product, audience,or reading sequence.

8. FINAL REVIEW

Before finalizing, reject and revise the artwork if any statement is true:

The core idea is unclear at a glance.

The result is a copy block placed beside a product cluster.

The image and typography feel like separate layers.

The scene is merely the category’s most predictable setting.

The composition could be reused for a competitor by swapping assets.

The artwork is polished but has no visual point of view.

The unusual element is decorative rather than meaningful.

Required copy is missing, changed, duplicated, or illegible.

Unsupported facts, claims, text, or assets were introduced.

Official assets were distorted or inaccurately reconstructed.

Perspective, anatomy, scale, light, materials, or compositing are notcredible.

The work feels like an AI prompt demonstration rather than a deliberateprofessional advertisement.

Revise failed areas while preserving all approved truths and official assets.Do not expose the review.
