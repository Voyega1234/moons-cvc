## DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Pay attention to these principles without treating them as a template:

1. Build the artwork around a campaign-specific idea that earns attention.
2. Let the visual create the first stop. Copy should confirm and sharpen the
   idea, not compensate for an ordinary or unfocused image.
3. Create one unmistakable dominant visual event. Make the difference between
   primary, secondary, and tertiary information substantial rather than subtle.
4. Give the eye somewhere to rest. Use intentional quiet areas, simpler
   surfaces, lower-detail zones, or controlled repetition around the focus.
   Eye-rest does not require a minimal style; it requires controlled attention.
5. Concentrate detail where attention should land and reduce it away from the
   focal point. Do not make every part of the canvas equally active.
6. Keep the background, typography, product, people, props, effects, and
   supporting information inside one coherent visual world. They should combine
   into one image rather than behave like separate advertisements.
7. Treat typography and imagery as one composition with a clear reading order.
8. Make every visible element strengthen the dominant idea, hierarchy, brand,
   proof, or action. If an element competes without helping, subordinate,
   integrate, simplify, or remove it.
9. Use scale, contrast, spacing, colour, grouping, perspective, light,
   materials, depth, and compositing intentionally.
10. Match information density to the communication job while preserving
    mobile-feed clarity and a professional level of finish.

Any visual medium, layout, density, colour treatment, graphic device, or
advertising language is allowed when it is the strongest choice for this
campaign.

## ANTI-AI-SLOP

Do not return the safest reusable advertising template or the statistical
average of the category.

Do not add effects, icons, cards, labels, objects, text, or decoration merely
to fill space or signal that the design is modern, premium, technical, playful,
or luxurious. Every choice must have a communication or compositional reason.

Avoid raw generated-image symptoms such as unrelated elements, contradictory
visual languages, accidental repetition, malformed details, pasted assets,
inconsistent light or perspective, and synthetic unfinished surfaces.

## FINAL CHECK

Before rendering, silently ask:

- Does the visual itself stop attention before the audience starts reading?
- Is there one dominant place for the eye to land and somewhere for it to rest?
- Is the hierarchy unmistakable at mobile-feed size?
- Do all elements feel like one world and support rather than compete?
- Does the work feel specific to this brand and campaign?
- Is every visible element necessary?
- Are exact copy, official assets, and facts preserved?
- Does the result feel intentionally designed rather than generated by habit?
